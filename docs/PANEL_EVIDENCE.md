# Panel evidence: where the DQN scheduler measurably wins

All numbers come from the ns-3 campaign (`results/ns3_campaign/`: 6 scenarios x 10 policies x 20 eval seeds, 300 s each) and
are regenerated by `tools/ns3_summarize.py` then `tools/panel_evidence.py` (full table: `summary/panel_evidence.csv`).

## Claim

DQN is a **criticality-aware** scheduler: it spends airtime on the urgent nodes and, on weak links, on the important node.
Against Round Robin it keeps urgent sensors inside their 200 ms deadline about 30% more of the time in every scenario.
Against the strongest heuristics (Max-Weight, CAG) it wins on the important node's deadline when links are weak.
It is not better on mean weighted AoI (3-8% worse than Max-Weight/CAG) and it pays for the gains elsewhere (see Costs).

## Tested and not significant (kept in the open)

- **Mean weighted AoI (the primary metric) vs Max-Weight/CAG:** DQN is 3-5% worse in S0-S3 (CIs exclude zero) and 8% worse in S4 (fault window, see Costs).
  In S1 vs CAG it is a tie (0.7% worse, CI includes zero).
- **S3 mobility, important-node deadline:** pooled DQN is 32% better than Max-Weight (14.2% vs 21.0% of time), but the deployed seed does not show it
  (it is 8% worse), so the gain depends on which training seed is deployed. It fails the robustness rule and is not claimed.
- **S5 mean weighted AoI:** DQN looks 16-26% better on average (0.262 s vs 0.357 / 0.314 s) but wins only 4-11 of 20 seeds and the CI includes zero, so the
  mean is driven by a few heavy-tailed eval seeds. Not claimed.
- **Mean AoI vs Round Robin:** 8-13% better in S0-S3, but only 14-28 ms, below the 50 ms practical floor.
- Fairness vs Max-Weight/CAG, urgent-node P99 vs Max-Weight/CAG: no significant gain.

## Costs and trade-offs

- **Urgent deadline vs Max-Weight/CAG:** DQN leaves urgent nodes past 200 ms *more* often (36-42% vs 30-39%; 3-6 pts more time past 200 ms than Max-Weight, 5-6 pts more than CAG),
  significant in S0-S3 and S5. The important-node gains above are paid for from here.
- **Round Robin is better on non-urgent deadlines:** important node past 500 ms 3.4% (RR) vs 16.1% (DQN) in S0; routine node past 1000 ms 0.6% vs 25.6%.
  RR spreads airtime evenly, so it only loses where the urgent classes matter.
- **Mean weighted AoI** is 3-5% behind Max-Weight/CAG (about 5-7 ms).
- **S4 (urgent node fails 60-90 s):** other nodes' peak AoI is 15.4 s under DQN vs 1.5 s (MW/CAG); recovery 0.67 s vs 0.63 s. DQN does not handle a dead node well.
- **Training-seed spread:** seeds 0 and 4 are about 10-12% worse than seeds 1-3 on S0 mean AoI.

## Method

- Metrics are the pre-registered ones (`docs/EVAL_PROTOCOL.md`): soft-deadline exceedance (200/500/1000 ms from `config/system.yaml`), tail AoI, Jain fairness.
- A claim is made only if (1) paired over 20 eval seeds: Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 (one family across all scenario x metric x baseline rows), bootstrap CI excludes 0,
  DQN wins at least 15/20 seeds; (2) the same holds for the deployed seed; (3) gain is at least 10% relative and at least 50 ms (age), 2 points (time past deadline) or 0.02 (Jain).
- Deployed seed = best mean weighted AoI on S0 (seed 1). Nothing else was used to choose it.
- S5 was calibrated from Round Robin runs only (-7 dBm gave 22.5% wasted slots in the probe, 23.9% over the full 20 seeds with all four nodes associated; -9 dBm drops STA-4 entirely). It was run once, with no tuning against scheduler results.
- Minutes per hour = point gain x 60. Mean AoI figures are from sampled 100 ms slots; one-way delay (about 0.4-0.7 ms) does not separate schedulers and is not used.
- Not covered: the Python-simulator sensitivity-sweep claim ("38 of 108 cells") has no data on disk and is not used.
