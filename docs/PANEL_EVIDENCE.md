# Panel evidence: where the DQN scheduler measurably wins

All numbers come from the ns-3 campaign (`results/ns3_campaign/`: 6 scenarios x 10 policies x 20 eval seeds, 300 s each) and
are regenerated by `tools/ns3_summarize.py` then `tools/panel_evidence.py` (full table: `summary/panel_evidence.csv`).

## Claim

The comparison set is four schedulers: DQN, Channel-Aware Greedy (CAG), Fixed Priority (FPQ) and Round Robin (RR).
DQN is a **criticality-aware** scheduler. Against Round Robin it keeps urgent sensors inside their 200 ms deadline about 30% more of the time in every scenario.
Against Fixed Priority it cuts weighted AoI by about 80% and keeps the important and routine classes far fresher, because Fixed Priority starves them.
Against CAG, the strongest baseline, it wins on the important node's deadline when links are weak. It is not better on mean weighted AoI (3-5% behind CAG in S0, S2, S3; level in S1)
and it pays for the gains elsewhere (see Costs).

Which baselines use criticality: CAG weights age by the class weights (10/10/3/1) and by delivery probability; FPQ ranks by class only; RR ignores class and age.

### vs Fixed Priority
Fixed Priority always serves the urgent class first, so it starves the other classes. DQN wins on every weighted and non-urgent metric in S0-S3 and S5, for example in S0:
weighted AoI 0.157 s vs 0.850 s (0.69 s, 82%), important node past 500 ms 16.1% vs 91.8% (75.8 pts), routine node past 1000 ms 25.6% vs 95.0% (69.4 pts), 20/20 seeds, deployed seed agrees.
The other scenarios are within a few points of these. The cost is on the urgent class (see Costs).

## Tested and not significant (kept in the open)

- **Mean weighted AoI (the primary metric) vs CAG:** DQN is 3-5% worse in S0 and S2 (CIs exclude zero) and 3% worse in S3 (not significant after correction); level in S1.
  In S5 DQN looks 16% better (0.262 s vs 0.314 s) but wins only 11 of 20 seeds and the CI includes zero.
- **Important-node deadline and mean age vs CAG in S0, S2, S3:** pooled DQN is 10-26% better (S3: 14.2% vs 19.1% of time), but the deployed seed does not show it, so the gain depends
  on which training seed is deployed. It fails the robustness rule and is not claimed.
- **Mean AoI vs Round Robin:** 8-13% better in S0-S3, but only 14-28 ms, below the 50 ms practical floor.
- Fairness and urgent-node P99 vs CAG: no significant gain.

## Costs and trade-offs

- **Urgent deadline:** DQN leaves urgent nodes past 200 ms more often than CAG (36-42% vs 30-37%; 5-6 pts in S0-S3) and far more often than Fixed Priority (36-42% vs 8-18%).
  The important-node gains above are paid for from here. If urgent deadlines were the only requirement, Fixed Priority would be the better choice.
- **Round Robin is better on non-urgent deadlines:** important node past 500 ms 3.4% (RR) vs 16.1% (DQN) in S0; routine node past 1000 ms 0.6% vs 25.6%.
  RR spreads airtime evenly, so it only loses where the urgent classes matter.
- **Mean weighted AoI** is 3-5% behind CAG (about 5-7 ms) in S0, S2 and S3.
- **S4 (urgent node fails 60-90 s):** other nodes' peak AoI is 15.4 s under DQN vs 1.5 s under CAG; recovery 0.67 s vs 0.63 s. DQN does not handle a dead node well.
- **Training-seed spread:** seeds 0 and 4 are about 10-12% worse than seeds 1-3 on S0 mean AoI.

## Method

- Comparison set: CAG, FPQ, RR. Max-Weight and Random were also run and are in the campaign CSVs, but are not part of the panel comparison.
- Metrics are the pre-registered ones (`docs/EVAL_PROTOCOL.md`): soft-deadline exceedance (200/500/1000 ms from `config/system.yaml`), tail AoI, Jain fairness.
- A claim is made only if (1) paired over 20 eval seeds: Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 (one family of 198 comparisons across scenario x metric x baseline), bootstrap CI excludes 0,
  DQN wins at least 15/20 seeds; (2) the same holds for the deployed seed; (3) gain is at least 10% relative and at least 50 ms (age), 2 points (time past deadline) or 0.02 (Jain).
- Deployed seed = best mean weighted AoI on S0 (seed 1). Nothing else was used to choose it.
- S5 was calibrated from Round Robin runs only (-7 dBm gave 22.5% wasted slots in the probe, 23.9% over the full 20 seeds, with all four nodes associated; -9 dBm drops STA-4 entirely). It was run once, with no tuning against scheduler results.
- Minutes per hour = point gain x 60. One-way delay (about 0.4-0.7 ms) does not separate schedulers and is not used.
- Not covered: the Python-simulator sensitivity-sweep claim ("38 of 108 cells") has no data on disk and is not used.
